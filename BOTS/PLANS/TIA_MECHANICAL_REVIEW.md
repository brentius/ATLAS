# TIA board mechanical review — 24 September 2026

The routed KiCad TIA is 35 × 12 × 1.6 mm. The five-pin connector at its right end is **J1**; **J2** is the single tip-wire solder land at the left end. KiCad 10.0.6 reports zero DRC violations, unconnected items and schematic-parity issues in the local, Git-ignored report `pcb/atlas_tia/routing_review/mechanical/mechanical_review_drc.json`. The local top render is `pcb/atlas_tia/routing_review/mechanical/review_top.png`; the [detailed design-plan review](TIA_DESIGN_PLAN.md#12-openstm-head-interface-review--24-september-2026) records the layout and decisions.

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Tip footprint | Keep the 1 × 2 mm, no-paste SMD pad for a short enamelled lead from the moving scanner. Provide fixed-end strain relief and a compliant loop; the pad cannot carry mechanical load. |
| Five-pin connector | Keep J1, the JST GH five-way header, as the selected ATLAS harness architecture. Its footprint spans about 10.75 mm of the 12 mm board width, and the mated plug/cable must exit the right edge. Check the cover opening, unmating access and cable bend before manufacture. |
| Mounting holes | OpenSTM's actual preamp PCB has **two plated, GND-net holes** with 1.6 mm drills and 2.8 mm copper pads, spaced 5 mm apart. This supersedes the earlier inference that its board had no holes. The PCB data does not itself label the holes as screw holes; confirm their mechanical role against the head drawing. Do not copy them into ATLAS until the head dimensions and GNDA-to-CHASSIS bond are decided; metal fasteners through grounded holes could join them. ATLAS's current hole-free outline still needs a defined support/clamp, or a hole pattern with rerouting and clearance checks. |
| Other release risks | Keep the metal cover off the exposed GNDA guard to avoid an unintended GNDA–CHASSIS bond. Verify the 1.6 mm board fits the chosen clamp; OpenSTM's published preamp is 1 mm thick. Fix the harness away from J1, check the mated pinout, and measure assembled feedback capacitance, noise and bandwidth. LTspice alone does not include these mechanical parasitics. |

This is a form-factor reference rather than a drop-in copy. OpenSTM's [native EasyEDA project](https://github.com/Dimsmary/OpenSTM/blob/main/PCB/ProProject_%E6%89%AB%E6%8F%8F%E9%9A%A7%E9%81%93%E6%98%BE%E5%BE%AE%E9%95%9COpenSTM_2023-09-09.epro) and [PreAmp Gerbers](https://github.com/Dimsmary/OpenSTM/blob/main/PCB/Gerber/Gerber_PreAmp_2023-08-25.zip) establish a **24.5 × 8.5 mm** board, two grounded plated holes near one end, and an FPC connector near the other. The [published assembly](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05413) uses fine soldered scanner wires, an FPC to its adjacent scanner board, and a metal preamp cover; the [JST GH family](https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/index.php?lang=2&series=105) is a locking wire-to-board system. The FPC is only the short internal head link: the adjacent `ScannerConnector` board takes coaxial power and output connections to the remote electronics. No PCB geometry or circuit value was changed during this review because the ATLAS head and cable envelope are not yet dimensioned.
